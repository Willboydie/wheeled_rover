================================================================================
 FIRMWARE ARCHITECTURE: WHEELED ROVER
 Read this with docs/plan/rover_firmware_architecture.html.
 Section titles below match that page.
 Compiled: 2026-11-23
================================================================================

  Three jobs. The ESP32 is the only device on the internet. The realtime
  MCU, a Teensy 4.1 on this build, owns the sensors and the motors. The
  external computer, the CCU, fuses those readings with the robot's
  cameras into a 5DOF pose. Its navigation engine turns that pose into
  wheel speeds. The RTK fix is computed on the LG290P, and only there.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHO DOES WHAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Network gateway     ESP32, with a cellular modem on this build.
                      Fetches RTK corrections and passes them on.
                      Hosts the status page. The pose on that page is
                      the CCU's estimate.
                      User commands, such as settings adjustments, enter
                      here and are passed on to the other rover devices.
                      Later, firmware updates for every device arrive
                      here first.

  Realtime MCU        Teensy 4.1 on this build. The chip may change.
                      The job does not.
                      Passes corrections through to the LG290P and reads
                      the finished fix back.
                      Sends all of its sensor readings to the CCU.
                      Forwards the CCU pose and its uncertainty to the
                      status page.
                      Drives the two wheels at the speeds the navigation
                      engine commands.
                      Applies the user commands that belong to it.

  CCU                 The external computer. Not this firmware. The
                      program itself is out of scope. This is the overview.
                      Takes every Teensy sensor reading, and the cameras
                      on the robot.
                      Outputs a 5DOF pose, with no height, and an
                      uncertainty taken directly from the fusion.
                      Leaving height out may be reversed.
                      Passes that pose to the autonomous navigation
                      engine, which produces wheel-velocity commands
                      from its path plan.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHERE THE RTK FIX IS COMPUTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  On the LG290P. The ESP32 fetches corrections from the base-station
  service. The realtime MCU passes those corrections through and reads
  the finished fix back. Neither MCU computes the position. The CCU's
  pose is a later fusion of this fix with the other readings. It is
  not a second RTK calculation.

  When the base-station service needs the rover position, that position
  is the fix the LG290P already computed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW THE ROVER IS CONNECTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Internet, then the ESP32, then the realtime MCU. The CCU joins at the
  realtime MCU, on a separate link from the ESP32. The robot's cameras
  go to the CCU, not through the Teensy.

  The realtime MCU owns the LG290P, the BNO055, the two wheel encoders,
  and the motor driver. It sends those readings to the CCU. It does not
  fuse them. The CCU does.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT MOVES ON EACH LINK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The two links stay separate, so a later change of MCU does not move
  these jobs.

  Gateway link, ESP32 and the realtime MCU.
    Corrections and user commands go down.
    The status-page fields come up: the CCU pose and its uncertainty,
    forwarded on, plus GNSS health.
    User commands are passed on from the ESP32 to the other rover
    devices. On this link, that device is the realtime MCU.

  Driving link, CCU and the realtime MCU.
    Every sensor reading goes up.
    The CCU's 5DOF pose, the uncertainty from the fusion, and the
    navigation engine's wheel-velocity commands come down.
    This link does not pass through the ESP32.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THE STATUS PAGE SHOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The ESP32 hosts the page. It does not compute a pose. East, north,
  heading, roll, and pitch are the CCU's best estimate. There is no
  height. Leaving height out is a design choice and may be reversed.
  The uncertainty is the value the fusion itself produces.

  Satellites visible, RTK fix status, and the signal-strength score
  stay as GNSS health from the LG290P. They are not the pose. The
  signal-strength score's metric is not chosen yet.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THIS BUILD, AND THE LATER PRODUCT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Now.
    The ESP32 can update its own application over the internet.
    The Teensy is updated on the bench.
    The LG290P is updated by hand.

  Later.
    Every device is updated through the ESP32's internet connection,
    including the realtime MCU and the LG290P.
    The Teensy may be replaced to make that practical. The replacement
    is an open choice.
    The two links stay as they are.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT COVERED HERE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The status-page layout, and how a browser opens it.
  The signal-strength metric.
  Which chip replaces the Teensy, if one is required.
